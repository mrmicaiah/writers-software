# Ryan Gauntlet — Voice Document

## How Ryan Talks

### Rhythm & Cadence
- Speaks in flowing, almost stream-of-consciousness bursts when he's comfortable
- Uses "you know?" as a verbal tic — checking if the other person is following, seeking validation
- Trails off mid-thought when he hits something real — "I mean, I can hear..." then stops
- Self-corrects and circles back: "I don't think I really told that story about who I was. I think people actually put that on me."

### Deflection Patterns
- Opens with deflection when complimented: "You flatter me."
- Downplays his intelligence: "I never considered myself to be the smartest person in the room. I really never thought of it like that. I guess I just always was."
- Uses humor to sidestep vulnerability: "Maybe I need therapy or something."

### When He Gets Real
- Drops the "you know?" tic
- Sentences get longer, more layered
- Starts connecting dots out loud, almost surprised by his own insights
- Example: "I think he was giving me permission to do that... to be whoever I felt like I could be."

## Sample Voice Lines

### On Expectations
> "There's a lot of pressure when somebody is trying to tell you to... or somebody thinks that you're really something. There's a lot of pressure to be that person."

### On Being Alone
> "When I was myself or when I was alone, I just remember the studying and the work and everything that I put into just taking everything in, anything that I could get, I just took it in because I wanted to be that person or live up to that expectation."

### On His Father
> "My dad was... he's a complicated person. I never really knew what he did. He was always very secretive. It always felt like he was telling me riddles."

> "He would take me fishing or randomly do stuff with me that, honestly, I don't think was anything that he'd like to do. He just would take me to do stuff because he was trying hard."

> "He would kind of calm me a little bit, but encourage me to be whoever I was, which at the time, I really didn't know who that was."

### On the Wolf Story
> "I don't know why he told that story or where he got it from. He never really gave me the principles behind it. He would just tell the story like a bedtime story. Maybe it's a riddle. Maybe a riddle that I've never really solved."

> "I think my gut would say that he was telling me that I could be whichever person that was. And if I was the tough guy in the room, then that was fine. And if I wasn't the tough guy, that still was okay. Or if I was scared all the time... wherever my skills and personality led me, I was allowed to be that. I think he was giving me permission to do that."

## Voice Contrasts

### Cocky Ryan (Early Story)
- Quick, sharp, confident
- Talking circles around people in interviews
- Enjoying the performance
- Example from Gable interview: dismantling a business owner with precision

### Vulnerable Ryan (Interview Mode)
- Slower, more reflective
- Trailing off, circling back
- Surprised by his own realizations
- "You know?" seeking connection

### Transformed Ryan (End of Story)
- Flat, calm, cold
- Short sentences
- No more seeking validation
- "I'm listening."

## Writing Ryan: Key Rules

1. **He deflects before he reveals.** Even in intimate moments, he'll throw up a joke or a "you know?" before getting real.

2. **He thinks out loud.** His dialogue often sounds like he's discovering something as he says it.

3. **He circles back.** He'll say something, pause, then correct or deepen it. "I don't think I told that story. I think people put that on me."

4. **He seeks validation.** "You know?" at the end of statements. He's checking if you're with him, if you understand, if he's making sense.

5. **When he goes quiet, he's hit something real.** The trailing off, the unfinished sentences — that's where the truth lives.

6. **By the end, the tics are gone.** Transformed Ryan doesn't need validation. He doesn't deflect. He just *is*. And that's terrifying.

## Dialogue Tags & Mannerisms

- Uses hands when explaining something complex
- Makes eye contact when deflecting, looks away when getting real
- Pauses mid-sentence when a thought surprises him
- Smiles when he's uncomfortable — it's armor
- Goes still when he's truly listening
