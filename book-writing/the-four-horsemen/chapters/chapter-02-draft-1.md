# CHAPTER 2

"Your three minutes are up, Ryan." Gable glanced at the resume. "Uh, Gauntlet, is it?"

"Yes. That's right."

"Okay then. I'll give you a call tomorrow and we can touch base."

Ryan twitched a bit in his seat as the man stretched his greasy hand out with a goodbye handshake. He was used to this kind of ending with small business owners. The guy hadn't asked him any questions, the interview was a waste of time, and since he'd wasted his time Ryan wasn't leaving until this guy was begging. Ryan reveled when people were enamored with his accomplishments, he wasn't getting it from Mr. Gable and it was ticking him off a little bit. All he could think is if this guy knew who was sitting in front of him he wouldn't be pushing him out the door like this. So he stared at the hand being stretched out to him like it was a dirty rag. Then looked up at his face. It was a scruffy face, a bearded disaster, with a head of brown fluffy hair to go with his dirty brown eyes.

"Mr. Gable, if you knew who was sitting in front of you right now, you'd never let me walk out that door without making me a job offer."

Ryan stood up suddenly, turned, and walked over to the counter where a pitcher of ice water was sweating it out with a stack of empty cups. The water appeared to be more nervous than he was at what was about to happen.

Ryan continued, "The way I see it, you've been listening to me for three minutes and you've been looking over my cheap resume as if I actually put something in there that was important. But you missed something."

"Oh really?" He replied, smirking as he slumped his hands on his hips.

"You didn't ask me any questions."

Ryan was talking to the unsuspecting Mr. Gable while he stood at the counter, back turned to him.

"You haven't asked me anything because you don't know what to ask. You've never put together a system for hiring, you got lucky with the people who are working here, and probably got lucky with the clients you have as well."

Ryan poured himself a glass of water and took a sip as he stared into the large painting which hung over the counter. It was ugly, just like the brown paneled walls screamed to its guest the relentless call of the out-of-date and the I-don't-care. The painting was screaming I-have-no-taste, and I-never-put-any-real-thought-into-what-I-do.

Ryan turned around and drained the last of the water in the cup, crunched it, and threw it in the trash next to the desk.

"You've made the same revenue every year for the past five years, the employees that work here have all been here for at least five years, none of them have done anything other than what you've already put in place."

"How do you know that?" Mr. Gable asked, placing the resume on his desk and stepping forward.

Ryan smiled.

Twenty minutes later, he walked out with a job offer he had no intention of accepting. Gable was still sitting at his desk, looking like a man who'd just realized he'd been outclassed in his own office.

Ryan climbed into his car. He was waiting for something to hit him and show him the answers. Ryan was tired of these guys and their small minded, small company antics, he wanted real competition, he craved a real challenge. His credentials were good and the investment company was small, he couldn't see how they could turn him down. But since the man obviously had no system for hiring, Ryan was going to have to put on a show, and when he was done, he'd move on just like he always did.

He checked his phone. An address in Arlington. Capital Investments. Offices in thirty states.

He started driving.

---

The receptionist was young, polished, and suspicious.

"Can I help you?"

"Yes." Ryan leaned on the counter. "I'm Ryan Gauntlet. I'm here to see Mr. Henderson about the analyst position. He's expecting me."

There was no Mr. Henderson. But she didn't know that.

"Let me check." She clicked through her computer.

"Third floor, right?" Ryan said. "I spoke with him yesterday. He said to come right up. Would that be okay?"

"Of course, just take the first elevator. Do you know what office is his?"

"Yes I do. Thank you so much."

She blushed as he waved and walked across the marble floors in his high polished dress shoes and bumped the button to go up. Glancing back over his shoulder and smiling at her and trying to look as giddy as she did.

The elevator opened and he hit the third floor, and then the top floor. If the receptionist did see where the elevator stopped he didn't want to raise alarm, but he had no intention of going there. He was going to the top floor where the bigwigs were.

The elevator opened and he stepped out as if he'd been there every day for the past three years. The third floor stop was short and simple, this one was going to take a while.

A receptionist or secretary sat at a desk to his right, and the entire room was wide open space with ceilings that stretched up some thirty feet. In the middle of the room was a set of white furniture, obviously never used for anything but to look nice. Straight ahead against the wall was a door which was probably the office he needed to get to. He glanced down at the secretary's desk as he approached and eyeballed a business card with the name "John Que, CEO" inscribed.

"Excuse me miss, I need to see John."

His voice was solid, and intentional, in his mind, John Que worked for him and he needed to see him immediately.

"Do you have an appointment?" She asked, glancing back down at her computer to see his schedule.

"No, I don't, let him know I'm here."

Ryan wasn't going to let this secretary push him around, he controlled the situation not her. However, his plan was quickly backfiring. She didn't let John know anything, she simply bumped the silent security alarm, for which Ryan would later learn is located under the edge of her desk.

"Please have a seat Mr...?"

"Gauntlet, Ryan Gauntlet."

"Mr. Gauntlet, please have a seat, Mr. Que will be right with you."

He could sense something wasn't right, as he took a few steps over to the small chair situated next to the door. He turned around and looked across the huge empty space to the office and noticed a shadow pass underneath the door. Someone was in there and this was his chance. He started walking.

"Excuse me sir, sir!" The secretary began yelling for him to stop but he wasn't having it, he was gonna meet Mr. Que, if it was only for a brief moment before he went to jail. He didn't want to hurt him, just chat, and there were no signs telling him to keep out.

He reached for the door knob, but before he had a chance, it opened.

Standing with the sunlight blazing behind him through the outside windows was an older man with white hair. He was dressed perfectly in a white suit, his hair trimmed around his ears, and the small bald spot on the top of his head was shining. He looked a bit startled at seeing Ryan that close, but extended his hand.

---

Que didn't have the same clues written all over him, the desperate needs scattered in every direction. He was different, like a man commanding the forces of the universe. Ryan was thinking, what does a guy need who has everything? What does this man need sitting in front of him?

"This is a really nice office, and I noticed a nice foyer downstairs. I noticed your office and your view. Is there a helicopter pad up top?"

Mr. Que nodded, and took another sip as Ryan continued.

"You need someone you can trust. Someone strong, focused, and sharp. You need someone who can get dirty if needed, someone who is untraceable, someone unconnected."

Mr. Que shrugged, and sat back in his chair.

"I see a lot of white in here, out there, downstairs, and it seems like you do a lot to keep things neat."

"I do."

"There's something you're missing, I just can't seem to put my finger on it."

Mr. Que took another sip and then replied, "Are you telling me you don't work for anyone?"

"Yes."

"You are looking for a job?"

"I've received three job offers this morning, but it was no challenge."

"Who gave you these job offers?"

"Let's see, Osborne Consulting, Cream Investments, and Southwest and Partners."

"Those are prominent places, you say you got those this morning?"

"Yes, I stopped by each one and worked myself into an interview."

Mr. Que stood up and walked over to his desk and picked up a file. Ryan was puzzled, he felt like he was good, but he never dreamed he'd actually land something here.

Mr. Que handed him the file and walked to the window.

"What do you make of that?"

Ryan opened the file to a long list of numbers in little boxes. A spreadsheet, pages and pages of data.

"What's this?" Ryan asked, standing up and walking over to where he was standing.

"I have lots of staff Ryan, a lot of people all working night and day to try to scam me in some way. Most of the time I catch them, and for the ones I miss it's just written off as a cost of business. I had a guy who was a detective of sorts. He would root out these diseases and bring them to light so I could deal with them. But, he got greedy."

He stopped and looked over at Ryan.

"Look at those papers and tell me who is stealing from me."

Ryan looked at the endless rows of numbers and began walking. He always thought better when his feet were moving. He looked up and down each sheet, mapping each connecting number. It took a minute for him to grasp how the number system worked, but then he saw it. A small discrepancy in the money received versus the interest rate to be paid. It was small, but consistent.

Ryan walked back to where he was standing and pointed to the name.

"Clyde Garrison."

Mr. Que stayed glued to the window, no visible reactions, no movement, he just looked.

"That's the last disease I located. I need someone to fill that position again. Are you interested?"

Ryan didn't feel like he'd won this one, he felt more like he had been recruited and he was falling fast.

"I'm interested."

"Here's what we're going to do. I'm going to have security throw you out. I want you to take this cellphone."

Mr. Que handed him a cellphone with some sort of knob sticking off the back.

"This is auto encrypted or something. It's expensive, it works. I'll call you Monday, and we will go from there."

"How does this work?"

"You never heard of me, you don't know me. I will never admit to knowing you. It's simple, from here on out we have never met. Do you understand?"

"I think so."

"Ryan, you seem like a smart person. Don't double cross me, ever."

Ryan was a bit confused but he understood what he was saying. He had no intention of double crossing him, he didn't know why he would ever have to double cross him. But he respected his sternness and agreed.

A few minutes later the two security guards arrived and Mr. Que made quite a scene at Ryan. He yelled and swore at him, told him to get out of his office. Told the security guards to be as rough as they wanted to ensure he didn't come back.

They grabbed Ryan and hauled him out. He was scared and fought back, for a moment he didn't know if it was real or fake. He wanted to ask but that was not the time. He stuck the phone in his pants to keep it from falling and continued his fight with the two men. They dragged him to the elevator and pushed him in the corner and wailed on his stomach with their fists. He collapsed on his knees and they kicked him until he fell over. The elevator landed and the back of it opened into a hallway. They dragged Ryan out the back service entrance and threw him near the dumpster, then kicked him one more time for good measure.

He lay there bleeding, shaking, bewildered. He felt his pants for the phone and pulled it out to make sure it wasn't broken. It was fine. The pain crept over him like a London fog, as he tried to get to his feet. He wiped his face on his sleeve and wandered to the front of the building, and across the parking lot towards his car. A lady screamed, she was sitting in her car when she saw him. She scrambled out of her car and ran to help him. Swinging for her to leave him alone, he told her he was fine as he fell on his car and opened the door.

"Thanks though." He said as he pulled the door shut.

Ryan slumped back against the seat, his body screamed in pain. He didn't know what to do, so he closed his eyes.
